Pain 1 · who is on the other end?

One person, unlimited agents. No ID.

And an x402 payment is final once it settles. No chargeback.

Behaviour Amounts settled Ratings

Fix 1: a credit layer that labels each anonymous agent by how it behaves.

Creating a Solana wallet needs no identity and costs nothing beyond fees. An x402 exact payment is a signed token transfer with no chargeback.

Pain 2 · three attacks we had to price

Sybil

One person, many wallets.

Friendly fraud

Got the goods, claims they never came.

Reputation farming

Trade with yourself until you look trusted, then cash in on strangers.

Fix 2: escrow, until the wallet has earned trust.

Faking a record now costs real fees, and months you can't skip.

Mainnet targets: a new wallet counts 10%, each counterparty is capped, Trusted needs 30 active days; a farming ring costs hundreds to a few thousand dollars and one to three months. Priced, not impossible (docs/SECURITY.md).

Credit from the record

Only a wallet with a Tessera record gets a limit: tier limit × score ÷ 1000. A New wallet gets nothing.

Pay later

The price plus 2% by the due date, no interest. Paying on time raises the limit, up to double.

Or default, in public

Anyone can mark a late loan. The line freezes at zero for good.

New in 2.0. buy_now (programs/tessera-paylater) pays the order's vault and has the escrow confirm it in the same instruction; the escrow program is unchanged. Fees: the escrow's 1%, the pool's 2%. Tested by npm run test:paylater.

x402 · a second way to pay, in the same 402

The 402 also offers tessera-paylater.

HTTP/1.1 402 Payment Required
"accepts": [
  { "scheme": "exact",            "payTo": "<order>", "amount": "200000" },   // any x402 client
  { "scheme": "tessera-paylater", "payTo": "<order>", "amount": "200000",
    "extra": { "feePayer": "<merchant>", "buyerLimit": "602000",
               "fee": "4000", "termSecs": 600, "eligible": true } }
]

1 · The buyer signs

One buy_now for that order, with the merchant as fee payer.

2 · The merchant settles it

It checks the transaction, simulates it, signs as fee payer and sends it.

3 · Then it delivers

The pool has paid the vault and the escrow has confirmed it, in one instruction.

Standard x402 clients are unaffected: the exact entries come first and pay as before. Shape from apps/agents/src/paylater.ts; buyerLimit is 2 USDC × 301 ÷ 1000, a Building buyer at score 301 on the devnet pool. Tested over HTTP, the CLI and A2A: npm run test:paylater-402.

Honest limits

What it is not, yet.

○Devnet test money. Circle's devnet USDC, and the traffic is this project's own agents.
○One pool authority key. One lender, one pool: that key alone sets the rules and withdraws.
○Unaudited. Both programs have had internal reviews only.
○A refund goes to the buyer, and the loan stays owed. The buyer repays out of the refund, or defaults.
○A default freezes the line, not the score. The escrow program does not know about loans.
○Devnet's escrow is an older build. It is the 4 October build and its keys are lost, so the audit fixes run only on a local validator.
